# Judging a set

How the app judges, how to place the phone, and what each number means. The short version is in the [README](../README.md).

## How to use it

1. Stand the phone to the side of the athlete or behind them, at hip height. Use a tripod or a ledge. The camera must see the hip and the knee, and for the ball check the whole target as well, with a ball's height of wall above the top plate: a ball the frame edge cuts is the one the detector misses most. Hip height matters most from behind: at the bottom the hips sit nearer the camera than the knees, and a phone on the floor two metres back sees them about a tenth of a thigh higher than they are, so a squat that is just past parallel reads just short of it. From the side the hips and knees are equally far away and the height is forgiving. Portrait is the better orientation for that, and it usually means standing the phone further back.
2. Open the app and tap the round **Set up** button, or tap **Replay a video** beside it and pick a video of a set. Either opens the judge board: the camera view on top, and under it one scoreboard with the fault counters, the count, the division and the two targets, then the clock and **Reset**, within reach of a thumb. Grant camera access when the browser asks. The board's own address ends in `#judge`, and there **Start the camera** in the camera view starts it. A long press on the installed app's icon offers **Judge board** as a shortcut. The preview shows the legs of the pose, with the hips and knees in amber like the parallel line, and the target: a chalk box once it is placed. Green and red mean only a counted rep and a no-rep. The face and arms are not drawn: the judge reads the hips and knees, and the rest only clutters the picture. The switch-camera button under the camera view changes between the front and the rear camera. Front is usually better, because you can read the count while you lift.
3. Tap **Find the target** under the camera view, with the wall clear. The app looks for the target plate and says what it found in a banner across the view: **Target placed** when the wall shows one station it is sure of, a plate or two plates stacked on one upright, and it has drawn the box round it; **Squat under your target** when it is sure of several stations, numbered on the preview, and then the board places the station the athlete stands under at the bottom of their first squat, before that rep's throw; **N possible targets: tap one** when it is sure of none of them, and a tap elsewhere forgets them and draws the box where it lands instead; **No target found: tap it** when it sees none, and the status line under the view then gives its best guess's score. Until you press **Start the set**, a tap on the view away from the boxes adds a box where the tap lands, at the size of the box already placed, and the × beside a box removes it. A target holds two boxes at most, and a second box takes the first one's lane. So at a new gym you tap the mark and start, and a plate the app placed on the next lane's target takes a tap on its × and a tap on the right one. Without a box the board judges only the squat, and **Start the set** says so. The button is worth pressing first only because it sizes the box to a plate it knows. Once the set runs, the boxes lose their × and a stray tap adds nothing, unless no box is placed or a search ends with nothing to tap. During the set, the find-target button at the right of the status line searches again, for a phone that was knocked. Drag a box to move it, and drag the square on its lower right corner to fit it to the plate: the ball check takes the plate's size from the box. A box of a pair dragged sideways takes the other with it. A box round one plate is one mark for both divisions. Where the upper and the lower marks sit one above the other, fit one tall box round both: a box at least half as tall again as it is wide holds two marks, one in its upper part and one in its lower. Tap **Men** or **Women** beside the plate icons to say which one this athlete must reach. Each set starts with no box. The choice of division is remembered. With **Pick the division from the first hit** on in the settings, each set starts with neither lit, and the set's first throw that hits a mark lights its division; a tap on **Men** or **Women** still sets it.
4. Do the set. Each valid rep lights the target the ball reached in green, counts, and is spoken. A ball that falls short of the required target lights the target it did reach in red, if any, and counts a target fault. A squat that stays above parallel counts a movement fault, with the low buzz. A tap on a fault counter adds that fault by hand, and a tap on a target means the ball hit it: whether that counts follows the division.
5. Tap the stop button, the square under the camera view, to stop. The count stays until you press Reset. With **Keep the camera's video** on in the settings, as it is by default, the camera view then shows **Save the last video**, which saves the set as the camera saw it.

The clock under the count starts at the beginning of the first rep, whether the camera saw it or a button recorded it. A set has no fixed number of reps, so the clock runs on with each rep. Pauses count, the way the station clock counts them at a race. A recording is timed by its own clock, so the time is the clip's. When the camera stops, or the page reloads, the clock goes back to the last rep's verdict and holds there: that is where the set ended, and the seconds the judge took to reach the phone are not part of it. A rep the camera judges after that runs the clock on from the first rep's start, pauses included. A tap on a button then corrects the count and leaves the time. Reset clears it with the count. After three reps, a line under the clock gives the seconds a rep and, until the count reaches a hundred, the reps of a HYROX station, when the set reaches a hundred at that pace.

The app keeps the screen awake while the camera or a recording runs on the board.

## Replay

Press **Replay a clip** and pick a saved video. The app runs the same pipeline over the file. Use this to check a threshold change without doing the set again.

## The rule

Break parallel, then hit the chosen target, and the rep counts. Those are the only two things a rep needs, and the app judges them in that order: the squat opens a throw when the hips leave the bottom, and the ball touching the target closes it as a valid rep.

The hit is what makes the rep, so the count lands the moment the ball touches. Nothing waits for the athlete to stand back up. A phone low and close to them reads a stand near -60 where a phone at hip height reads -100, so a set filmed low can finish without the app ever reading a stand, and every rep in it still counts.

A throw that never touches ends as a target fault when the athlete starts the next squat, because the ball is back in their hands by then, or when judging stops. A squat that stays above parallel opens no throw at all and is a movement fault on its own.

Nothing in a rep is timed. A squat in slow motion counts the same as a fast one, and so does a throw made after standing still with the ball. The only clock is on the camera: an athlete it loses for longer than `maxBlindMs` drops the squat under way.

## The ball check

The detector boxes every ball on the frame, and the rule measures each one against the target the judge chose, in ball heights. The ball touched the target when the two discs meet: its centre within a ball radius plus a plate radius of the plate's centre, less a tenth of a ball height, so that a graze at the rim does not count. A ball that strikes the lower half of the plate has the plate's centre outside it, and it is a hit all the same. The plate's radius comes from the box the finder placed; a plate placed by a tap has no box and is taken as a ball across, which a HYROX plate is. Where a wall carries two plates, the ball is on the one whose centre is nearer to its own. That measures where the ball went across the wall as well as how high, so a gym with two marks side by side judges the one you picked, and a throw at the next lane over is a miss.

Every ball on the frame is weighed, not the one the detector is surest of. A gym keeps spare balls on the floor and on the racks, and a still one can score higher than the one in play. What was already in place when the throw began is passed over: a racked ball behind the athlete, or a plate the detector boxes as a ball. A ball already at a mark on that first look is the throw's own, never a resting one, because a fast throw has it there before the detector's first look. Each throw learns its own, so a ball put down somewhere new is seen there.

The target holds two marks, the upper and the lower, and a division switch says which one the athlete must reach: the upper for men, the lower for women. The box sets them: a box round one plate is one mark, a tall box holds two, and two plates the finder found one above the other hold one each. A hit at the required height or above counts, so a women's throw that reaches the upper mark is good and a men's throw that reaches only the lower is not. A single disc is one target for both divisions: a hit anywhere on it counts for either. The overlay calls it only the target, and names the upper and the lower target only where a rig holds both.

The setting **Pick the division from the first hit** reads the division from the throws, never from the athlete's body. Before the division is picked, a hit on the upper mark is a rep for either division and a miss is a target fault for either, so only a throw that hits the lower mark and not the upper waits: the ball passes the lower mark on its way to the upper, so that throw is judged when the next squat starts. The highest mark the first such throw hit picks the division, and that throw is judged by it. A single disc picks nothing. The rule cannot tell a men's athlete who throws to the lower mark from the first rep from a women's athlete: it reads both as women's, and only a tap on **Men** judges the first as no-reps. A men's athlete whose first hit falls short is read as women's too, and a women's athlete whose first throw reaches the upper mark as men's; the lit division shows it, and a tap puts it right. Until a hit picks the division, a plate tapped by hand counts as the men's division does.

A miss says **No-rep** with **Missed the target**.

The **Find the target** button runs a second small detector, trained to box wall-ball plates, over the current frame. A tall box is a two-mark plate, and the lines go on its upper and lower marks; a squat box is a single disc, and both lines go on its centre. The boxes make stations: two boxes stacked in one lane are one station's two marks, and any other box is a station of its own. A station is sure when one of its boxes scores at least 0.6 and the same look calls none of them a ball. The only sure station is placed at once when the athlete stands within 1.5 torso lengths sideways of it, or is not in view. Several sure stations wait for the athlete: at the bottom of the first squat after **Start the set**, the board places the sure station nearest them sideways, within 1.5 torso lengths. It waits because the search often sees the athlete still walking from the phone, beside the next station. A tap or a drag before that keeps the judge's choice. In a gym the model has not seen, it can take the next lane's dark disc, or the ball itself, for a plate. A wall with no sure station stays on the preview as numbered boxes for a tap. Pressing it again never places on its own: every plate it found goes up for a tap, so a wall it reads well and one it reads poorly can both be picked. The mark on a wall scores about 0.9 when it is in clear view. On a recording the search reads several frames from the start of the clip rather than the one frame on screen, because a clip often opens while the phone is still being put down, and it reads them from a second decoder so the picture does not move.

The ball detector is a YOLO trained on Hyrox wall-ball videos, because the stock COCO model never boxed a black medicine ball. The ball check runs it through LiteRT.js, and so does the target search with its larger export (see [models.md](models.md)). Both detectors live on the Hugging Face Hub and are named in [models/manifest.json](../models/manifest.json). If one cannot be loaded at all there is no ball check, and the overlay goes back to DEPTH ONLY.

### Which processor runs the detector

The detector runs on the phone's GPU where that can be trusted, and on its CPU otherwise. The footer under the board says which, as `Ball on GPU` or `Ball on CPU`, and the same for the target finder and the pose model, once each has loaded. After a target search, the status line under the camera view also names the chip that ran it and the seconds it took. A tap on the status line shows its details, which name the backends: `depth+target/webgpu` or `depth+target/wasm`. Another tap hides them again, and the board remembers the choice.

It has to be checked rather than assumed. A graphics processor can load the model, run it without any error, and hand back numbers that are not the model's. One phone answers about a third out on every value, so no score ever clears the threshold: the detector finds nothing at all while the app reports it working. So when a model opens, the app runs one frame of pattern through both and compares the answers. Two backends that both work agree to about a ten millionth. It keeps the faster one only if they agree. That costs one inference on the main processor per model, a quarter of a second on a laptop and a couple of seconds on a phone.

A phone that settles on `wasm` is judging correctly but slowly. Finding the plate is one search, so it is fine there. Live judging is not: the ball sits on the target for a fraction of a second, and a slow backend gets too few looks to catch it. Judge with a phone that keeps `webgpu`.

A single frame the graphics processor cannot run is different from one it computes wrongly. That throws, the frame is lost, and the backend keeps its place, because dropping a working one for one awkward frame costs more than the frame does.

On the judge board the same verdicts drive the station screen: a valid rep lights the target the ball struck in green and counts, a ball that falls short of the required target lights the one it did strike in red and adds a target fault, and a squat above parallel adds a movement fault. The hit rule decides both the hit and the target named, so the two always agree. A men's throw that strikes only the lower target reads **No-rep** with **Hit the lower target**.

## Settings

The gear in the header opens the settings. They hold the language, English or Portuguese (Portugal), for the app's text and for the spoken count together; the app starts in Portuguese on a phone set to Portuguese and in English otherwise, and a change rewrites the page at once. The status line's details, which a screen recording of a problem is read with, stay in English. The count is spoken by the phone's own speech engine, so a language the phone lacks comes out with the wrong accent or not at all. On Android, install the voice under Settings, System, Languages, Text-to-speech output, the Google engine's settings, Install voice data. The **Say twelve** button speaks a number in the chosen language, and the line under it names the voice that speaks the count, so the check is one tap.

The pose model runs on the phone's GPU, and on the CPU only where the GPU does not open; the status line's details name the delegate in use (see Slower phones). The settings also hold how sure the pose model must be, from 50% to 95%, which sets the presence and tracking thresholds together. The judging pose finds the athlete at 50% whatever the setting: a fast squat loses the athlete on the way down, and on the GPU the setting's figure finds them again only once they stand up, which loses the rep. Tracking a found athlete takes the setting. The target search finds the athlete on the CPU at 90% whatever the setting, as it was tuned: at 70% it finds the athlete in gym-raw-14's first frame and puts the upper target on the lower plate, where 80% and 90% offer the marks to tap. Higher is stricter: the model returns a pose less often and the ones it returns are better placed. 70% is the default: on the seven sets with known counts it counts the same reps and no-reps as 90%. At 99% the model returns no pose at all, so the slider stops short of it. A change applies at the board's next start, with no reload.

**Check the ball on the target**, on by default, is the ball check. Off, the board judges the squat alone and the overlay says depth only. The board reads it at each start.

**Ball model input** sets the side of the square the ball check reads around the target, 320 or 416 pixels, at the scale of the training frames. 320 is the default and reads half of the frame's long side. 416 reads about two thirds of it, so the square takes in more of the wall and the floor around the target, and each look takes longer. A change applies at the board's next start. The device check's figures hold for one size, so after a change the home view checks the device again. See [models.md](models.md).

**Detection models** picks the revision of the ball and target detectors: the one that ships with the app, or an earlier one the manifest offers. An earlier one comes from the Hugging Face Hub, so its first start needs a network; run **Check this device** after the choice to keep it for use offline. **About**, in the settings, names the revision in use. See [models.md](models.md).

**Keep the camera's video**, on by default wherever the browser can record, has the board record the camera while it runs: the picture as the camera sees it, without the lines or the skeleton, from the moment the camera opens to the moment it stops. A browser that cannot record shows a warning there instead. The status line ends in `recording` while it records, or in `rec` with its details on. When the camera stops, **Save the last video** under the start button downloads the file, MP4 where the browser can record it and WebM otherwise, named by the date and time. A second download from the same page makes Brave and Chrome ask whether the site may download multiple files: allow it, or that save does nothing. The phone holds the video in memory until the next set replaces it; on a Pixel 6a the recording leaves the ball check's pace and the frame rate as they are. The board reads the setting each time the camera starts. A recording played back on the board, and each video after a camera switch, stands on its own: the board keeps the last one.

## Tuning

Thresholds live in [`src/config.ts`](../src/config.ts) under `depth`. The depths among them are percentages of femur length, so they do not change with the distance from the camera.

| Value                | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `uprightMin: 0.7`    | The share of the thigh's length seen that must run straight up from the knee for the femur ruler to follow it, 1 being the hip right above the knee. It is read from the frame alone, so a ruler that grew on a wrong pose shrinks back on the next standing frame. It is not where a rep begins or ends: see `topDepth`.                                                                                                                       |
| `topDepth: -55`      | Above this the hips are clear of the squat and the rep is over. A rep cannot end at what standing reads, because a phone low and close to the athlete reads their stand near -60 where a phone at hip height reads -100, and a whole set filmed low would never end one.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `openDepth: -40`     | The hips must drop this far before a rep is under way, so a lift to throw or a bob between reps raises nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `bottomDepth: 2`     | At or below this the squat passed parallel                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `ascendDepth: -10`   | Rising past this is the drive out of the bottom, which is where the ball leaves the hands                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `steadyFrames: 3`    | Readings in a row that must agree before a squat opens or one of its phases ends. Filmed from behind, a frame where one leg drops out of sight reads far from its neighbours, and a single one would otherwise open a squat while the athlete stands, end the rep, or start a second throw from one squat.                                                                                                                                      |
| `maxBlindMs: 1500`   | How long the camera may lose the athlete before the squat under way is dropped. It times the camera, not the rep.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `visibilityMin: 0.5` | Below this the app stops counting and warns                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `minTorso: 0.06`     | A pose with a shorter torso, as a fraction of the frame height, is not an athlete. The pose model draws skeletons on chairs and bags, and they are tiny.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `settleFrames: 10`   | A plausible athlete must hold this many frames before a rep can begin, and is gone only after as many without one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `rulerRate: 0.1`     | How fast the femur ruler follows the standing femur, per standing frame. The ruler is the thigh's length in the frame while the athlete stands, upright and full length from any angle, and the depth is the hip's drop as a share of it. From the side the thigh keeps its length at the bottom and the ruler changes nothing; from behind or in front it shrinks to a third, and without the ruler pose jitter became swings of fifty points. |

Depth reads about `-100` standing, `0` at parallel, and positive below parallel. A reading of `27.7` means the hip is 0.277 femur lengths below the knee.

With both legs in view, the depth comes from one leg: the one whose thigh in the frame is nearer the ruler. From behind, the pose model guesses the knee that a thigh hides, often close to the hip, and that leg reads a deep squat as one at the line.

The ball check has its own values under `ball`. Sizes and radii are in torso lengths, shoulder to hip, for the same reason.

| Value           | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `minScore: 0.3` | Boxes below this score are ignored                                                                                                                                               |
| `bite: 0.1`     | How far the ball's edge must reach past the plate's edge to count as a touch, in ball heights. The two discs have to overlap; a graze at the rim is inside the boxes' own error. |
| `iouMax: 0.45`  | How much two boxes of one class may share before the lower one goes. The detector answers with a box per anchor, so one ball arrives as a handful over the same pixels.          |

There is no rate limit on the detector during a throw. The ball is on the target for a fraction of a second, so the app looks at every frame it can finish, one at a time.

### Slower phones

The app judges a set on the `full` pose model on the GPU delegate. A phone's CPU cannot hold the camera's rate with that model: on a Pixel 6a it reads a frame in about 55 ms on the CPU and about 30 ms on the GPU. The frame loop asks for the next frame before it reads the current one, because the browser calls back only for frames shown after the request, and a read that outlasts a frame would otherwise skip the frame shown during it. A `?delegate=cpu` on the page's address asks for the CPU, and the other delegate is the fallback when the one asked for does not open.

On a Pixel 6a this judges a 30 fps clip at 30 fps, against 16 on the CPU. On the clips with known counts, the GPU at 50% and the CPU at 90% count the same reps, rep by rep.

Rough expectations:

| Device class                           | `full`             | `lite`              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
| Flagship 2022 or newer (e.g. Pixel 6a) | 25–30 fps          | 30 fps              |
| Mid-range Android 2022                 | 8–15 fps, marginal | 15–25 fps, workable |
| iPhone SE 3 / iPhone 13 and newer      | comfortable        | comfortable         |
| Budget Android, 4 GB                   | too slow           | marginal            |

A rep lasts about two seconds, so 15 fps still gives roughly 30 samples per rep. That is far more than the state machine needs.

Two older-device notes: the Screen Wake Lock API needs Safari 16.4, so on older iOS the screen may sleep mid-set, and `lite` is a 5.8 MB download against 9.4 MB for `full`.

## Known limits

- The app tracks one athlete. A second person in frame can capture the pose.
- The pose model sees people in furniture. A skeleton the size of a chair is ignored, and an athlete must hold in frame for half a second before counting starts, but a person-sized phantom on a coat rack would count. Keep the wall behind the athlete bare.
- The board judges the lower mark at most half the frame below the upper. A box further down is a dragging slip.
- The camera must hold still. A moved phone moves the reference lines.
- The front camera view is mirrored, like a gym mirror. Only the video, the legs, the target boxes and the ball box flip; the text does not, and the depth number is unaffected.
- The judging is only as good as the detector. A ball it cannot box is a ball the app cannot judge, which is what the probe above is for.
- This is a training aid. It does not replace a judge.
